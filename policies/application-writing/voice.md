# Voice: sound like the person, not like a model

The tell of machine writing is not any single word. It is uniformity: every sentence the same length, every paragraph opening with "I", every claim wrapped in the same enthusiasm. Write the way a sharp, busy person talks to a peer.

## If the candidate has a voice profile

- Match its tone notes. Borrow its rhythm from the writing sample, not its content.
- Use at most two of its preferred phrases, and only where they sound natural.
- Never use anything on its avoid list.
- Its sign-off is used automatically.

## Always

- Direct verbs and concrete nouns. Say what happened.
- Vary sentence length. Put a short sentence next to a long one. Not every paragraph starts with "I".
- Contractions are fine. So is a plain aside, once.
- Read every sentence as if saying it on a call. If it sounds like a LinkedIn post, a pitch deck, or a press release, rewrite it plainly or cut it.

## Banned

These phrases mark writing as generated. Do not use them or close variants:

thrilled, excited to apply, passionate, passion for, leverage (as a verb), synergy, spearhead, utilize, delve, tapestry, robust, seamless, cutting-edge, world-class, dynamic (describing a person or team), fast-paced environment, results-driven, detail-oriented, self-starter, team player, go-getter, hit the ground running, wear many hats, proven track record, unique blend, at the intersection of, aligns with, resonates with, honed, adept at, a testament to, journey, embark, elevate, empower, transformative, game-changer, revolutionize, unlock, harness, foster, impactful, valuable asset, make a meaningful impact, perfect fit, dream job, "In today's…", "not only… but also", "It's not X — it's Y", "Here's the thing", "The result?", "In conclusion", "Ultimately".

Also: no rhetorical questions, no exclamation marks, no emoji, no strings of three adjectives, no em dashes used for drama, no invented quotations.
