# Positioning and initial customer profile

## Positioning statement

For iPhone-first people in an active, repeat-application job search, **RoleDawn** is the evidence-backed career operator that keeps working after they log off. Unlike job boards, autofill extensions, and black-box auto-apply tools, it finds fitting roles, shows the source behind every material change, asks for approval in the user's messages, and produces a receipt for what was actually submitted.

## Category and promise

Category: **career agent** or **job-application agent**.

Acquisition promise:

> Your job search has a night shift.

Trust contract:

> Nothing invented. Nothing outside your rules.

Product explanation:

> RoleDawn finds fresh roles, builds evidence-backed applications, asks for your approval by text, and shows exactly what went out.

Do not lead with “AI-powered,” “agentic,” model names, ATS count, or application count. These are mechanisms or proof, not the reason to buy.

## Primary job to be done

> When my search requires dozens of similar applications, help me stay early and consistent without spending every evening on forms or risking what gets sent under my name.

Functional jobs:

- Monitor sources continuously.
- Filter by hard constraints before writing.
- Tailor from real evidence.
- Reuse approved exact answers safely.
- Surface only material decisions.
- Complete repetitive ATS work.
- Confirm and track every submission.

Emotional jobs:

- Let me sleep without feeling I missed the window.
- Reduce shame and exhaustion from repetitive rejection labor.
- Make delegation feel reversible.
- Preserve my voice and professional reputation.

## Launch ICP

### Required behaviors

- Active search in the next 30 days.
- At least ten plausible standardized applications per month.
- Uses an iPhone in the United States.
- Comfortable with a text-driven approval loop.
- Has a resume and enough genuine evidence to tailor.
- Feels the time cost and the reputation risk.

### Likely demographic concentration, not a hard filter

- Roughly 22–34 years old.
- Zero to eight years of experience.
- Tech, data, product, design, sales, customer success, operations, marketing, or finance role families.
- Recently laid off, graduating, or actively moving from a stalled role.

### Segment priority

| Priority | Segment | Why now | Risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Early/mid-career professionals, 2–8 years | Strong evidence base, meaningful urgency, better willingness to pay | Search volume can be lower than new-grad cohorts |
| 2 | New graduates | High repetition, high referral density, high AI comfort | Lower willingness to pay and thinner evidence |
| 3 | OPT/STEM-OPT candidates | Acute timing and eligibility-filter value | Sensitive authorization logic and existing competitor ownership |
| 4 | Career changers | High need for evidence mapping and taste | Targeting is ambiguous and forms are less repeatable |

## Anti-ICP for the MVP

- Wants thousands of indiscriminate applications.
- Expects guaranteed interviews or employment.
- Will not review or define sensitive answer policy.
- Needs regulated licensing, executive placement, clearance, or bespoke portfolio review.
- Wants the service to invent credentials or evade portal controls.

## Differentiation pillars

### 1. Evidence before eloquence

Every generated claim points to a Career Vault fact or source passage. RoleDawn does not turn a plausible inference into candidate history.

### 2. Authority is earned

The default is review. A user may later grant narrow, expiring rules to a proven adapter and application class. The agent never broadens those rules itself.

### 3. Failure is a product moment

“Workday asked for a CAPTCHA. Your answers and files are saved. Tap to finish.” is better than a spinning task or a false success.

### 4. Receipts beat activity feeds

A status update says work happened. A receipt identifies the company, role, canonical source, answers, files, versions, timestamp, portal result, and confirmation evidence. Provider references remain available as secondary evidence when useful; they are not the primary label.

### 5. Outcome quality over quantity

Optimize eligibility, fit, approval rate, recruiter response, and interview yield. Cap volume before the service becomes spam.

## Objection handling

| Objection | Answer |
|---|---|
| “Will it lie on my resume?” | No unsupported claim can pass the evidence validator. You see material changes and their sources. |
| “Will it apply anywhere?” | It follows your salary, location, role, sponsorship, employer, and daily-cap rules. MVP submissions require approval. |
| “What about sensitive questions?” | Saved exact policy or live approval only. Optional demographic fields are never inferred. |
| “Do recruiters know?” | The submitted materials and contact identity are yours. RoleDawn does not promise invisibility or evade employer rules. |
| “What if the site changes?” | It pauses, preserves work, and asks for takeover. It does not guess through a new certification or CAPTCHA. |
| “Why not use an autofill extension?” | Autofill waits for you to find and operate each form. RoleDawn runs the durable workflow and brings you only the decisions. |
| “Why not use a high-volume auto-applier?” | RoleDawn is built to protect truth, fit, and verifiable outcomes instead of maximizing a raw counter. |

## Messaging hierarchy

1. Outcome: the search keeps working overnight.
2. Control: approve from the thread; inspect anything material.
3. Truth: evidence-backed claims and voice fidelity.
4. Proof: application receipt and outcome tracking.
5. Breadth: supported role sources, ATS families, and surfaces.
6. Technical mechanism: models, browsers, adapters, and cloud workflow.

## Claims we may make at launch

- “Builds a ready-to-send queue while you sleep” if the alpha actually does.
- “Nothing is submitted without your approval” while mandatory approval is the product behavior.
- “Every material claim is linked to your Career Vault” after the validator and UI exist.
- Named ATS support only after repeatable fixture and live-canary gates pass.

## Claims we may not make without evidence

- “Guaranteed interviews,” “gets you hired,” or any outcome guarantee.
- “Works on every ATS,” “fully autonomous,” or “never fails.”
- Employer logos, aggregate placement claims, or response-rate lifts without consent, sample size, time window, and outcome definition.
- “Secure,” “compliant,” or “private” as a blanket claim; name specific controls and certifications instead.
- A vendor relationship or endorsement not granted in writing.
